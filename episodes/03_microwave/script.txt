In 1945, a man invented a new way to cook because a chocolate bar melted in his pocket.

His name was Percy Spencer, and he had never even finished school.

But he built radar parts for the war, and he was brilliant at it.

One day, standing next to a humming radar tube, he felt something sticky.

His chocolate bar had turned to goo.

The room wasn't hot.

He wasn't hot.

So he sent someone out for a bag of popcorn kernels.

He held them near the tube, and they burst all over the lab.

Next, he tried an egg.

It wobbled, shook, and exploded right in a coworker's face.

Meanwhile, the war was ending, and his factory's thousands of radar tubes were about to become useless.

You see, that radar tube was firing out invisible microwaves.

And microwaves make the tiny water molecules inside food spin back and forth, billions of times a second.

All that jiggling turns into heat, right inside the food.

That's why the snack got hot, but the room didn't.

So his company turned a war machine into a kitchen machine.

The first one was taller than a man and weighed as much as a piano.

It cost about five thousand dollars.

Today, almost every kitchen has one.

Not bad for a guy with no diploma and a ruined snack.

And it all started when...
