In 1956, a man grabbed the wrong part from a box, and his machine started beating like a heart.

His name was Wilson Greatbatch, and he was building a gadget to record heartbeats.

He reached for a tiny resistor.

But he grabbed one a hundred times too strong.

He soldered it in, switched it on, and froze.

The circuit went quiet, then pulsed.

Quiet, pulse.

Quiet, pulse.

Once every second, just like a heart.

Meanwhile, doctors had patients whose hearts kept slowing down until they stopped.

The only machines that could help were as big as a TV and plugged into the wall.

If the power went out, the patient was in serious trouble.

You see, every heartbeat starts with a tiny electrical spark.

In those patients, the spark wasn't getting through.

And Wilson's mistake made the exact spark a heart needs, once every second.

So he shrank it until it fit in the palm of your hand.

He quit his job and built them in the barn behind his house.

In 1960, surgeons placed one inside a seventy-seven-year-old man.

His heart beat steady again.

Today, millions of people walk around with one in their chest.

All because of one wrong part from one box.
