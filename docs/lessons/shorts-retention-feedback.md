Short 01 (38 s, published 2026-10-19) analytics: only 40% stayed (60% swiped in the first 1 to 2 s), average view 13 s (36.8%), drop-off around the 10 to 15 s mark where the montage turns into the boss/work theme. YouTube's advice: tease the twist/conflict inside the first 2 seconds ("it wouldn't make you productive, it would trap you"), tighten the setup-to-twist transition, trim pauses or add dynamic visual/sound cues at 10 to 15 s.

**Later numbers (YouTube Studio AI summary, pasted 2026-10-21):** 627 views, 267 engaged views, 39.04% stayed (60.96% swiped), average view 19 s = 50.07% of 38 s, 9 likes, 1 subscriber, 0 comments. Retention stabilised at about 45 to 50% after 10 s. Advice: open with the catch/stakes, cut the setup to 4 to 6 s, and END WITH A DIRECT QUESTION to start comments (e.g. 'Would you take this deal?').

**Why:** the user wants Shorts to hook viewers both visually and vocally, not just be well made. This pairs with their earlier notes (no "POV", story from the first word, no title/chips, quiet sfx, Cutie Molly captions).

**How to apply:** every Short opens with a staccato premise plus an immediate "but"/trap line in the first 3 s (guide 4.3 formula), a striking moving first frame, a voice delivered with urgency (no slow start, no 0.5 s lead-in gap); put the first turn by about 8 s; a loop-opener line around 12 to 15 s; end with a spoken question that invites comments; keep gaps 0.25 to 0.35 s except one or two deliberate holds after the turn; a dynamic sound/visual cue at the mid-point. Track the next Short's retention against these numbers. See [[no-recurring-characters]] for the story rule.
