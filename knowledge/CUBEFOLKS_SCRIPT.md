# Cubefolks script template (from the owner's sample, `docs/CUBEFOLKS_STYLE.md`)

A first-person kid tells a strange story about their town, as plain fact. Original and made-up. Check every script against
`knowledge/SHORTS_PLAYBOOK.md` before voicing (speaker `R`). Length 35-60 s at **4.5 words a second** (160-270 words),
`gap_default` 0.08-0.1: the sample has one pause over 0.35 s in 61 s.

## The shape (percent of runtime; the sample's line in brackets)

| % | Beat | What it does | The sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | **The rule** | The town's strange rule as fact, in line 1. "In this town / In my town ..." is the house opening; vary it after a few Shorts ("Every house on my street ..."). | "In this town, there's a shape-shifting mimic." |
| 3-18 | **How the rule works** | 3-5 short lines that build it, repeating a word ("it ... it ... it"); one limit or catch. | "If it eats you, it becomes you. It looks like you, it talks like you..." "But it can only be one person at a time." |
| 18-30 | **The routine** | How often it happens and what the town does about it; ends on the unknown. | "Every few weeks, someone in town vanishes... We just never know who." |
| 30-45 | **One named neighbour as proof** | A name, a time, two sensory details, a warm habit the narrator remembers (care before hurt). | "Last March, it was our neighbor, Mr. Greene... his coffee was still warm. He'd waved at me from his window every day." |
| 45-52 | **The community scene** | The whole town gathers (church, gym, town hall, block party) and fails. | "The whole town met at the church... But nobody could." |
| ~52 | **Re-hook** | The narrator starts to notice. | "After that, I started noticing little things." |
| 52-80 | **Three clues from three named people** | Each person does one odd thing, with a **quoted line** in the narration. One clue links back to the proof neighbour (a song, a phrase, a habit). One clue points at someone close (mom, a sibling). | the crossing guard: "If you're still you."; Mrs. Kerr whistling Mr. Greene's song; mom stops chopping: "Lots of people know that song." |
| 80-90 | **Confirmation + creepy flashback** | The rule fires again on one of the clue people; a memory turns sinister in hindsight. | "Three weeks later, Mrs. Kerr vanished... she'd kept me after class, closed the door, and asked if I ever got scared at night." |
| 90-100 | **The turn on the narrator + loop** | The community scene again, and everyone turns on *me*; the last words are a cliffhanger fragment that runs into line 1. | "The whole town went back to the church. And when I looked up, everyone in the room was staring at me. Then, all of a sudden, [In this town...]" |

Rules: concrete everyday places (street, school, church, kitchen, crossing, lawn); named grown-ups with a title (Mr., Mrs.,
Coach, Pastor, Officer); kid words; no explanation of the twist (the viewer connects the clues); at least two clues planted before
the turn; the turn by 85-90% (this genre's turn is a cliffhanger, so the tail after it is under 3 s); a loop line, never a moral.
Every line has a picture with a character DOING something (see the motion library): write actions into the lines (waved, chopping,
whistling, leaned in, closed the door, looked up).

## Fill-in prompt (for ChatGPT or for us)

> Write a 45-55 second YouTube Short script (about 210-240 words, one sentence per line) told by a 10-year-old about their town,
> first person, past tense, as plain fact. Original and made-up. Follow this shape exactly: (1) line 1 states the town's strange
> rule; (2) 3-5 short lines explain how the rule works, with one catch; (3) how often it happens and that nobody knows who/when;
> (4) one named neighbour it happened to, with a date, two sensory details and a warm habit; (5) the whole town gathers somewhere
> and fails; (6) "After that, I started noticing little things."; (7) three clues from three named people (one is family), each
> with a short quoted line, one linking back to the neighbour; (8) the rule strikes one of them, plus a creepy memory of them;
> (9) the town gathers again and everyone turns to stare at the narrator; (10) end on an unfinished fragment ("Then, all of a
> sudden,") that loops into line 1. Everyday words, no hedging, no moral, no "subscribe". Every line must show someone doing a
> visible action. Under it: the 2 clues that point at the narrator, a 2-4 word pop-in for frame 0, an on-screen question for the
> last 3 s, a pinned comment, and 3 titles under 50 characters that keep the twist.
